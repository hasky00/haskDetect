# Case 001 — Update, 3 November 2026

**Where the Binance loot is, five days on: three parked piles, $2 billion stranded, and a live exit through THORChain.**

Written 3 Nov 2026, 21:15 CET. Chains: Ethereum, Avalanche C-Chain, Bitcoin. Sources: Blockscout public API, mempool.space public API, thorchain.net explorer, Arkham Intelligence entity view. Every address and hash below is public and checkable. No proprietary data.

This update extends the original Case 001 file. It does not replace it.

---

## Summary

The attacker split the haul into three behaviours:

1. **Parked.** The ETH was never sold. 214,720 ETH (~$491M) sits in two fresh wallets with zero outgoing transactions. A third pile, 29,934 wBETH (~$76M), is stuck because redeeming it goes through Binance.
2. **Stranded.** The big stablecoins and gold tokens were never moved out of the loot wallet. USDT, USDC, XAUT, USD1, PAXG, FDUSD and EURI, about **$2.08B**, are either frozen or one issuer decision away from frozen.
3. **Laundered.** Everything small-cap and liquid was dumped into ETH through Uniswap over 36 hours, then went quiet on 31 Oct 08:17 UTC. On the evening of 3 Nov a separate Avalanche leg started cashing out to Bitcoin through THORChain, in 1–2 BTC lots, every few minutes.

Arkham now clusters the whole operation as entity **Binance Hacker** (`binance-hacker-2`): 9.9k addresses, $5.75B across chains. Net to exchanges so far: $36.6M, 92% of it through Chainflip.

## 2. The live exit: Avalanche → THORChain → Bitcoin

Found at 20:00 UTC on 3 Nov while reading the Arkham entity page; verified on thorchain.net and mempool.space.

### Pattern

Fresh unlabelled Avalanche addresses fund four hacker C-Chain wallets in 9k–58k AVAX chunks. Each wallet sends 9k–19k AVAX ($75K–$170K) to `THORChain_Router` every 2–5 minutes. THORChain swaps AVAX for BTC at ~0.00011 BTC/AVAX with 0.2% slippage, settling in about 20 minutes. Swaps that overshoot the slippage limit are refunded in AVAX (observed: 1,221 AVAX back to `0xe130…`). The cadence is scripted.

The BTC lands on a fresh `bc1q` collector per sender. Collectors are reused two or three times, then swept to a second hop: either a fresh parked address or a shared hub.

### Sample swap, end to end

- THORChain tx `115098376E47AEB3DC53BEB766428F033717FF51A04843D87E2C8E3A40AB777C`
- 19,024 AVAX from `0x2a828d0b500d93abf98f135fa5d750634c062029` at 20:22 UTC
- 2.0702 BTC to `bc1qa5n7qryprtttd65nm4tjyq4gkvnglf79t92dxr`, settled 20:43 UTC
- Bitcoin tx `92407BE2EF3DA9240D68F56D61324A56FC4C8E368CEAF0C2F1C5F6383E8B7828`
- Fees paid: $268 (0.16%)

Block 28,520,589 on THORChain shows the footprint: BTC pool 2.34 RUNE and AVAX pool 0.43 RUNE in liquidity fees for one six-second block, with every other pool at dust. The laundering was large enough to dominate the network's per-block fee split.

## 4. What a detector would add

Signals from this phase, on top of the six in the original file:

07. **Parked-then-pulse.** Four days of total silence on the big piles, then a scripted drip on a side chain. Silence on the headline address is not the end of the case.
08. **THORChain pool-fee anomaly.** A single chain's pool dominating per-block liquidity fees on a cross-chain DEX is visible in public block data before any address is labelled.
09. **Collector rotation with a hub.** Fresh `bc1q` per swap, reused 2–3 times, converging on one consolidation address. The hub is the choke point; the collectors are noise.
10. **Stablecoin stranding.** The attacker never tried to move USDC, XAUT or PAXG. They knew they were freezable. What an attacker leaves behind tells you what they expect issuers to do.
